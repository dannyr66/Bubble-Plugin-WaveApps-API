Five hooks for Rally

The video you sent teaches five ways to open a SaaS ad on Meta, plus two rules for the first three seconds. This page applies all of it to Rally and mobile party operators. It covers the plan, five scripted ads with mock-ups, and how we could turn them into real videos with you and Chris.

01 · What the video teaches

A "meet the platform" opener isn't a hook

The video opens on a fake ad that reads "Meet the all-in-one platform for modern finance teams." It calls that a nametag: the viewer skips it in under half a second because it says what the product is and nothing about them. It then walks through five hook types that work for B2B SaaS on Meta right now, and says to run all five because each one reaches a different buyer.

HookWhat it doesExample from the videoFunnel
1 CircumstanceDescribes the viewer's exact moment so precisely they think "that's me.""It's the third day of month-end close, you're still matching invoices by hand, and your CFO just asked why the numbers don't tie out."Cold
2 Real reasonNames the true cause of the pain. You teach the mechanism first, then show the product fixing it."The real reason your close takes ten days isn't your team. It's that your data lives in four tools that don't talk to each other."Cold
3 Price tagSays the price in the first line. It scares off people who could never buy, so the leads you do get already know the number."$20,000 a year. Here's what you get."Know you
4 Receipt"Just finished setting this up for a customer," then a real screen share. It plays like a case study, not an ad.Screen share of the actual dashboard and workflow.Warm
5 Proof dropOpens on a specific customer result. A hard number buys about ten more seconds if it's true and you can back it up."Nx customer result," specific and real.Warm
Rule

Two hooks in three seconds

The on-screen text should say something different from the spoken line. Muted viewers get one hook and viewers with sound get two. If the text only repeats the caption, it adds nothing.

Rule

Bin the nametag and feature list

"All-in-one platform" and a list of features both go in the trash. Neither one gives the viewer a reason to stop scrolling.

Tactic

Comment-keyword CTA

The video ends with "Comment OPENER and I'll send you the doc." The comments push reach up and the DMs give you a lead list.

Format

Explainer plus reactor

One character teaches while the other stands beside him reacting. That's the role you and Chris would fill. The video uses Family Guy characters, and we can't use those, so we'd use your faces or your own cartoon versions.

02 · What this means for Rally

Rally's headline is a nametag right now

Our own words: "All-in-one command center for mobile entertainment businesses."

That's exactly the line the video throws away. The idea behind it is good, but it's in the wrong spot. "Rally replaces the 3–5 tools you're stitching together" is a strong Real reason hook once we tell it from the operator's side. Keep "Rally your crew. Run your day." as the closing line on the end card. It shouldn't be the opener.

Who we're talking to: owner-operators of foam parties, game trucks, mobile laser tag, bubble shows, escape trailers and similar businesses. They work from a phone, usually with 1–3 crews, and run bookings out of a Google Form, a calendar, Square or Wave, a PDF waiver and a group text. Their worst hours are Friday night and Saturday morning.

What they feel (the circumstance): chasing deposits by text, a waiver unsigned at setup, a crew member who doesn't know the address, a mom asking "what time are you coming?" at 11 p.m.

Why it happens (the real reason): booking, payment, paperwork and crew live in separate apps, and the operator is the cable connecting them.

Proof we already have: PA Fun Company runs five brands on Rally. Every proof-drop and receipt ad should use real numbers pulled from that account.

03 · Cast

You and Chris as the two characters

The skeptic · Chris Asks what operators are thinking
  • The voice the operator groups already trust
  • Asks the doubtful questions: "I've done this for years with a Google Form. Why switch?"
  • Delivers the circumstance hooks, because he has lived every one of them
  • Solo version: posts in his own voice in the foam-operator groups
The explainer · Danny Teaches, then shows the screen
  • Founder who also runs five brands on Rally day to day
  • Takes the real reason, receipt and proof drop hooks
  • Records the screen shares on his phone from real bookings
  • Explains how it works first, then sells

You can swap the roles. What matters is that one person teaches and the other reacts the way the audience would. That pairing is why the source video holds attention through a slow topic.

05 · Storyboards

Beat by beat

Each ad runs 30–45 seconds: hook, then teach, then show Rally, then the CTA. Every ad ends on the same card so the brand builds up over repeat views.

1 · Circumstance Cold

0–3s · Hook

Chris, tired, holding his phone: "It's Friday night, you've got four parties tomorrow…"

Sticker: 4 parties tomorrow. 0 deposits confirmed.

3–12s · Twist the knife

"…one waiver's unsigned, and your new guy doesn't know the address." Danny next to him nods: "Been there."

Quick cuts of text bubbles stacking up.

12–30s · Show

Danny opens Rally: "Here's my Friday now." Action items list, three green dots on every booking.

Screen recording, zoom on the dots.

30–40s · CTA

"Comment SATURDAY and I'll send you the exact reminders we automate."

End card: Rally your crew. Run your day.

2 · Real reason Cold

0–3s · Hook

Danny: "The real reason you're doing admin at midnight isn't that you're disorganized."

Sticker: It's not you. It's your 5 apps.

3–15s · Teach

"Your booking's in a form, the money's in Square, the waiver's a PDF and your crew's in a group text. You're the cable between them." Chris: "…yeah, that's me."

Five tiles, a red × on every connection.

15–32s · Fix

"In Rally, one booking does all of it: the invoice goes out, the waiver goes out, crew gets assigned, reminders get scheduled."

Tiles snap into one Rally hub.

32–40s · CTA

"Teach first, sell second." Learn more, or start a trial.

End card.

3 · Price tag Retarget

0–3s · Hook

"Rally is [$XX] a month. Here's exactly what you get."

Sticker: Costs less than one party a month.

3–20s · List

Booking page, deposits and auto-billing, waivers on phones, crew portal, 19 automations, client portal.

Each item checks off over the matching screen.

20–30s · Compare

"Add up what you pay now for forms, e-sign and texting. Then add your Friday nights."

Their stack added up against one Rally line.

30–35s · CTA

"Free trial. Your booking link is live today."

Show only to site visitors and video viewers.

4 · Receipt Warm

0–3s · Hook

Danny on face cam: "I just set up Saturday's foam party for a customer. Watch what happens when she books."

Sticker: Real booking. Real Saturday. No edits.

3–20s · Walk through

A real booking comes in, the deposit invoice sends itself, the waiver link goes out, and the crew gets a text.

Phone screen recording with real taps. Blur the client's name.

20–30s · Payoff

Crew portal on event day: address, map, gear list, the "On my way" button.

Three dots turn green.

30–38s · CTA

"That's the whole admin side of a party. Learn more."

End card.

5 · Proof drop Warm

0–3s · Hook

"[X] bookings last season across five brands, and I never chased a waiver by text."

Sticker: 5 brands. 1 app. 0 waivers chased.

3–15s · Back it up

Show the real dashboard: revenue by brand, signed documents count, [avg days to payment].

Real screenshot, nothing mocked.

15–30s · Why

"Waivers attach to the service, so they send themselves. Deposits block the booking until they're paid."

Chris: "So you just… stopped chasing?"

30–38s · CTA

"Comment SATURDAY and I'll send you our setup."

End card.

06 · Plan

Where each ad runs

Cold
Circumstance + Real reason

Meta Reels and Feed. Target interests around party rental, inflatables, event entertainment and small business owners, plus lookalikes of current Rally users. Make one circumstance version per niche (foam, game truck, laser tag, bubbles), since each one reaches a different operator.

Warm
Receipt + Proof drop

Anyone who watched 50% or more of a cold ad, commented, or visited the site. Chris also posts these organically in the operator groups.

Know you
Price tag

Site visitors, trial starters who haven't connected Stripe, and anyone who commented SATURDAY. These people already know you, so the price filters for real buyers.

Lead magnet for "Comment SATURDAY"

"The Saturday Playbook": the automations we actually run (booking confirmed, deposit reminder, day-before, day-of crew text, review request), copied out of Rally's 19 defaults with our real wording. Send it by DM. Every commenter becomes a lead for the price-tag retargeting.

First four weeks

Week 1Film
  • One afternoon: all five hooks, two takes each
  • Record the receipt on a real booking
  • Pull real numbers for the proof drop
Week 2Test
  • Five ads, same budget each (for example $15/day each, about $525 for the week)
  • Same body and CTA, only the hook changes
Week 3Cut and copy
  • Stop the bottom two hooks
  • Make three new circumstance versions of the winner, one per niche
Week 4Retarget
  • Turn on the price tag for warm audiences
  • DM everyone who commented the playbook

What to watch

Hook rate> 30%

3-second views divided by impressions. This tells you whether the first line works.

Hold rate> 10%

ThruPlays divided by 3-second views. This tells you whether the teaching part holds.

Cost per commentTrack

Each SATURDAY comment is a lead. Compare this across hooks.

Shows who actually set up, as opposed to who only clicked.

07 · Making the videos

Can we generate these?

Mostly yes, but the receipt and proof-drop ads need real footage of Rally, because the video's whole point is that those have to be true. Here are three ways to make them, from cheapest to most produced.

Recommended first

Film you two + motion graphics

You and Chris talk to a phone camera, and the screen recordings come from real bookings. I build the stickers, captions, app tiles and end cards as animated graphics in code, then combine them with your footage.

  • Most trusted by operators
  • Around a one-afternoon shoot
  • Required for ads 4 and 5
Closest to the reference

Cartoon versions of you two

Illustrated Danny and Chris characters standing beside animated diagrams, like the source video. You record the audio and the animation is built in code to match it.

  • Needs original character art, no Family Guy
  • Fits ads 1–3 best
Most scalable

AI avatar of you or Chris

A tool like HeyGen or Synthesia builds a digital twin from about two minutes of footage, and ElevenLabs clones the voice. Then you can make new niche versions from a script alone.

  • Only with your own consent and footage
  • Label it as AI where Meta requires
  • Keep it off the receipt ad
